# Today hero cards — the design calls

`CLAUDE.md` carries the constraints a change has to respect. This is the reasoning behind them, from the 2026-08-14 rebuild and the device pass that followed.

- **The artwork lives in `src/assets/` and is imported, not put in `public/`**, so Vite fingerprints it (`card-ongoing-7HkdvLEt.jpg`). A `public/` file keeps its name forever, and the service worker would happily serve a stale one after an art change. Sources are `ongoing attack card image trimmed.png` (the untrimmed `ongoing attack card image.png` is kept alongside it) / `attack free card image.jpg` in the repo root; the shipped copies are re-encoded to 800px JPEG q65 (1.9 MB → 62 kB for the ongoing one), which is invisibly lossless on flat vector-style art and matters for an offline-first PWA that bundles everything.
- **The hero is not a card, as of 2026-08-25.** It was a rounded `bg-bg-surface` panel inset in the page padding, which meant a hard-edged image box inside a hard-edged card — two frames around the one thing on Today that should read as the top of the screen rather than an object on it. It now bleeds past the page padding (`-mx-4 sm:-mx-6`) and up to the top of the scroll region (`-mt-5`), and **Today renders no `TopBar` while a hero is on screen** so the artwork runs under the status bar. The hero's label and headline are the page's heading in that state; the first-run empty state has no hero and keeps the bar.
- **The blend works because the hero has no background of its own.** Both gradients fade from `bg-bg-base` — the page colour itself — so the artwork dissolves into the page instead of into a card tone that then has to meet the page at a seam of its own. They fade to `bg-bg-base/0`, never `transparent`: interpolating to `rgba(0,0,0,0)` greys the mid-stops. Tune the stops, not the image: this app is read mid-migraine and contrast under the headline beats showing more picture.
- **Three gradients, one per edge the image actually has.** Across (`from-bg-base from-40%`) covers its left edge; down from the top and up from the bottom cover the edges the card's corner radius used to hide. The right needs nothing — the image reaches the viewport there.
- **The horizontal gradient's opaque stop must sit *past* the image's left edge.** The image is a hard-edged box (`w-[64%]`, right-aligned, so it starts at 36%); wherever the gradient has already begun fading at that edge, flat page colour meets artwork in a single step and a seam runs straight down the screen. The opaque stop is 40% for exactly that reason — move the image and the stops move with it.
- **The vertical fades are long and carry a mid stop.** A short linear fade finishes with a visible kink: the eye reads the point where it reaches zero as an edge of its own, which is the thing the fade is there to avoid. Seen at `h-[18%]` on the top edge and fixed at `h-[30%]` with a 45% mid stop.
- **The artwork stops short of the very top** (`calc(0.75rem + env(safe-area-inset-top))`). It ran to y=0 and under the status bar, which put the clock and the battery on top of the picture. The gap is small on purpose — it should read as the artwork starting below the status bar, not as a band of chrome above it. The inset is added, never substituted: it reads as 0 in the browser preview.
- **The band's *height* is what sizes the artwork, not its width.** The sources are square and `object-cover` scales to cover, so with the box taller than it is wide the height drives the scale. A hero that looks too zoomed wants a shorter `min-h`, currently `19rem`.
- **The text block is centred against the artwork** (`items-center` on the band), not top-aligned in it. Top-aligned against a full-height image, the text read as text that happens to have a picture behind it rather than as one composition; centring also keeps the two aligned however the band grows at larger text sizes.
- **How well it blends still depends on the artwork's own background.** The attack-free art is near-black and disappears into the page completely; the ongoing art carries a cool navy plate that stays visible as a lighter, bluer field where the gradient has faded. That is the picture, not a seam — but it is the reason a replacement image should be dark and warm at its edges.
- **Only the text block is width-limited (`max-w-[64%]`), not the content column.** An action needs the full width, and being a solid shape it reads fine over the artwork where body text would not.
- **The attack-free hero has a greeting and no detail line** (2026-08-25). "Since Thu 20 Aug, 10:40" under "8 minutes" was the headline's own figure said a second way, in the most valuable space in the app; the date is still on the attack in `AttackDetail`. The freed line went to `greeting()` — which is also where the "Hello" that `TopBar` used to carry on Today ended up. Both small lines sit above the headline and read as one sentence. **Not on the ongoing hero**: a greeting above "Ongoing attack" would be the app failing to notice.
- **The hero carries only an action the FAB can't express** (2026-08-25). "Log an attack" and "Add update" were removed: each was the nav FAB's own action repeated in a second, louder place, which put two solid-accent targets in one viewport on a screen where accent means *press this*. `End attack` is what's left, and it stays `btn-secondary` rather than taking the vacated primary slot — promoting it would rebuild the competition the change removes. The attack-free hero carries nothing at all. The first-run empty state keeps its labelled button: a bare plus is a weak first affordance.
- **The headline is `whitespace-nowrap` and overflows that limit on purpose.** "Started 24h 38m" broke onto two lines against the 64% width and pushed the card taller; the headline is the one line that has to land at a glance, and running it over the artwork costs nothing when the buttons already do. Checked at the `xl` text scale, where it stays on one line and still clears the card's right edge.
- **Which edge of the artwork survives the crop is per-card (`imageAnchor`), not a global choice.** `object-cover` always overflows the box horizontally, so one side is trimmed either way, and the right side to keep depends on where the subject sits in that particular source. The ongoing card's art is **pre-trimmed tight to the swirl** (`ongoing attack card image trimmed.png`), so `right` keeps the subject hard against the card edge; the attack-free art is a full square with the moon left of centre, so `left` is what carries the moon into the visible strip instead of burying it under the gradient. Counter-intuitive part worth remembering: keeping the *left* edge pushes a centred subject *right*, because the trim comes off the other side.
- **Spacing is to a supplied spec, and the gaps are deliberately uneven:** 24px left/right and 32px top/bottom padding (`px-6 py-8`), then 4px under the label, 16px under the headline, 24px before the buttons. The label is a kicker attached to the headline, so it sits tight to it; the detail line is a separate fact, so it doesn't. A uniform stack left the label floating between the two. The action buttons are `rounded-xl` (12px at the default text size, and the radius used by ~36 other buttons in the app) rather than `rounded-full`.
- **The image is `alt=""` + `aria-hidden`**: it carries mood, not information, and every fact on the card is in the text beside it.
- **The ongoing card's route to `AttackDetail` is its text block**, via `HomeCard`'s optional `onOpenDetail`. The whole card was the tap target before, which stops working once buttons live inside it (and nesting a button in a button is invalid); a separate "Details" button was tried and dropped as clutter on a card whose job is to be glanceable.
- **Severity is stated plainly as "Pain severity: 9"**, not colour-coded. The old severity-tinted number and border read as part of the artwork once there was artwork behind them, and keeping the detail line the same shape as the attack-free card's is what makes the two interchangeable at a glance.
- `formatSinceLong` ("14 days") backs the headline, separate from `formatSince` ("14d") which is still right for dense rows elsewhere.
